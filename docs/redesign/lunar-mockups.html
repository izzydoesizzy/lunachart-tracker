Step 5 of 6

Peak Day — the key concept

Peak Day is the last day of peak-quality mucus — clear, stretchy, wet, slippery. You identify it the day after, when quality changes or mucus disappears. In Justisse, count four days after Peak before considering the fertile window closed.

Practitioner tip. You won't know it's Peak Day on the day itself. Be patient — this retrospective call is how the method earns its accuracy.

Check your understanding

Monday: egg-white, 3+ cm, slippery. Tuesday: tacky and dry-ish. When can you first identify Peak Day?

Exactly. Peak Day is Monday — but you can only call it on Tuesday, once the change shows. Now the 4-day count begins.

Not quite. On Monday you can't yet know it's the last peak day. The identifying moment is the day after, when quality drops. Try again.

Glossary

Every term, in plain language

ChartingBasic Infertile Pattern (BIP)

Your personal baseline on infertile days — consistently dry for some, habitual cell slough for others. Any change from it is your point of change. Taught in Skill 4.

MucusPeak Day

The last day of peak-quality mucus (clear, stretchy, wet, slippery). Identified the day after, when quality drops. Justisse counts 4 days after it.

ChartingPoint of Change

The first observation different from your BIP — the day your fertile window opens.

TemperatureCoverline

A reference line you draw 0.05 °C above the highest of the six temperatures before your shift. Three temps above it help confirm ovulation has passed.

MucusCell slough

Pasty, crumbly, white-yellow discharge. Commonly confused with fertile mucus — if it's habitual and doesn't change with your cycle, it may be part of your BIP. Ask your practitioner.

MethodThe double-check

Justisse confirms ovulation only when both the Peak Day count and the temperature shift agree. Cross-checking is what makes symptothermal methods so effective.

MethodHRHP

Holistic Reproductive Health Practitioner — a trained Justisse educator. Recommended, especially for pregnancy avoidance. Find one at justisse.ca.

CycleLuteal phase

The days between ovulation and your next period, driven by progesterone — the reason your temperature stays high.

No terms match — try "peak" or "BIP".

Myth busting · 8 min

"Isn't this just the rhythm method?" — No. Here's the difference.

For skeptics, partners, and anyone who got the 1950s version in health class.

The rhythm method predicts future fertility from past cycle lengths — a calendar guess. Fertility awareness methods observe today's biomarkers: cervical mucus, waking temperature, cervical position. One extrapolates; the other measures.[1]

[1] Peragallo Urrutia R, et al. Effectiveness of Fertility Awareness–Based Methods for Pregnancy Prevention. Obstetrics & Gynecology. 2018;132(3):591–604.

Used with instruction, modern symptothermal methods reach perfect-use effectiveness comparable to hormonal contraception — with typical use, the range is wider, which is exactly why this app keeps telling you to learn with a practitioner.

Related lesson: Skill 2 · Temperature — why the shift confirms, never predicts.
